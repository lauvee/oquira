# Dominican Open Finance Exploration Framework (DOFEF)

**Version:** 0.1\
**Status:** Foundational framework\
**Project:** Oquira\
**Geographic focus:** Dominican Republic\
**Scope:** Open Finance, financial infrastructure, digital financial services and emerging financial models.

## 1. Purpose

The Dominican Open Finance Exploration Framework (DOFEF) defines the research, experimentation and innovation scope that guides Oquira.

Oquira aims to explore how financial information, institutions, technology providers and individuals could interact through more connected, interoperable and accessible financial services.

The Dominican Republic is the primary context for this exploration.

However, the project is not limited to financial products, technologies or regulatory frameworks that currently exist in the country. It also investigates international implementations, emerging business models and experimental financial services that could inspire future possibilities for the Dominican financial ecosystem.

Oquira is an independent, open-source experimental and educational initiative. It does not represent an official implementation of Open Finance in the Dominican Republic.

## 2. Strategic vision

Oquira seeks to become a practical laboratory for understanding and experimenting with the future of financial services in the Dominican Republic.

Its central thesis is:

> Understand the Dominican financial ecosystem, learn from international experiences, explore new possibilities and transform relevant ideas into practical experiments.

The framework combines three complementary dimensions.

### 2.1 Dominican financial reality

Understand the existing financial ecosystem, including:

- Traditional financial institutions.
- Regulatory and supervisory institutions.
- Fintech companies and technology providers.
- Payment systems and financial infrastructure.
- Consumers, businesses and merchants.
- Existing financial products and services.
- Current digital transformation initiatives.

### 2.2 International learning

Investigate implementations and practices from other countries, including:

- Open Banking and Open Finance.
- Financial data aggregation.
- Consent management.
- Payment initiation.
- Digital identity.
- Interoperability standards.
- Embedded finance.
- Real-time payments.
- Financial data ecosystems.
- Artificial intelligence in financial services.

International implementations are references for learning, not automatic blueprints for the Dominican Republic.

### 2.3 Experimental future scenarios

Explore financial models that may not currently exist in the Dominican market.

Examples include:

- Financial services connected across multiple institutions.
- Portable financial identities.
- Interoperable cooperative financial services.
- Programmable payments.
- AI-powered financial assistants.
- Financial services embedded in commerce platforms.
- Autonomous financial agents.
- New models of financial data exchange.

These scenarios are exploratory. They do not imply regulatory approval, commercial availability or institutional adoption.

## 3. Scope of exploration

The framework covers the broader financial ecosystem rather than focusing exclusively on banks.

### 3.1 Financial institutions

Research should consider different types of financial institutions and their respective roles.

Initial areas include:

- Commercial banks.
- Savings and credit banks.
- Savings and loan associations.
- Credit corporations.
- Public financial institutions.
- Financial cooperatives.
- Credit unions and community financial organizations.

The project must distinguish between different institutional categories, legal frameworks and supervisory responsibilities.

### 3.2 Financial authorities and institutional environment

Research should consider the roles of relevant Dominican institutions, including:

- Banco Central de la República Dominicana (BCRD).
- Superintendencia de Bancos (SB).
- Superintendencia de Pensiones (SIPEN).
- Superintendencia de Seguros (SIS).
- Superintendencia del Mercado de Valores (SIMV).
- Instituto de Desarrollo y Crédito Cooperativo (IDECOOP).
- Other public institutions relevant to financial infrastructure, consumer protection and data governance.

The purpose is to understand the ecosystem, not to suggest that all these institutions share the same supervisory responsibilities.

### 3.3 Fintech and emerging participants

The exploration must include existing and potential non-bank participants:

- Digital payment providers.
- Electronic wallets.
- Remittance providers.
- Lending platforms.
- Personal finance applications.
- Financial data providers.
- Digital identity providers.
- Insurtech companies.
- Wealthtech platforms.
- Regtech solutions.
- Financial infrastructure providers.
- Technology companies offering embedded financial services.

### 3.4 Consumers and businesses

The framework must consider different user segments:

- Individuals and households.
- Micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (MIPYMES).
- Large businesses.
- Merchants and colmados.
- Independent professionals.
- Cooperatives and community organizations.
- Financially underserved populations.
- Dominican citizens living abroad and cross-border users.

### Layer 1 — Institutional simulation

Represent financial institutions and participants through simulated environments.

Examples:

- Bank Simulator.
- Cooperative Simulator.
- Fintech Provider Simulator.
- Payment Provider Simulator.
- Insurance Provider Simulator.

### Layer 2 — Interoperability

Explore how independently simulated systems exchange information.

Examples:

- REST APIs.
- OpenAPI specifications.
- Financial data models.
- Standardized account and transaction resources.
- API discovery.
- Event-driven integration when justified.

### Layer 3 — Identity, consent and trust

Experiment with:

- OpenID Connect.

- Delegated access.

Build demonstrations of services that use the simulated financial ecosystem.

### Layer 5 — Intelligence and automation

- Agent-based interactions.

## 8. Research classification

Every relevant finding, idea and scenario must be classified to avoid confusing reality with experimentation.

### Fact

A statement supported by an identifiable source.

Example: An official institution has announced a specific initiative.

### Hypothesis

An assumption that requires research or validation.

Example: Financial data interoperability could improve access to financing for MIPYMES.

### Proposal

A potential model, solution or approach that Oquira wants to explore.

Example: A shared API directory for simulated Dominican financial providers.

### Simulation

A technical implementation that demonstrates a hypothetical or conceptual model.

Example: A simulated cooperative sharing account information with an authorized application.

These classifications should appear in research documents and scenario specifications whenever appropriate.

Each experiment should follow a common documentation structure.

1. Scenario ID and name.
2. Dominican context.
3. Problem or opportunity.
4. Target users.
5. Participating actors.
6. Current documented situation.
7. International references.
8. Experimental hypothesis.
9. Proposed experience and workflow.
10. Financial information involved.
11. APIs and integrations.
12. Identity and consent requirements.
13. Security and privacy considerations.
14. Oquira simulation requirements.
15. Limitations and assumptions.
16. Evaluation criteria.
17. Potential future extensions.

This template ensures that each experiment connects research, product design, architecture and implementation.

## 10. Research and development methodology

Oquira will progress through six stages.

### Stage 1 — Exploration

Understand the Dominican financial environment and international reference models.

Deliverables:

- Financial ecosystem map.
- Institutional landscape.
- Research findings.
- International model comparisons.
- Opportunity inventory.

### Stage 2 — Product discovery

Identify meaningful opportunities and define experimental scenarios.

Deliverables:

- Scenario portfolio.
- User journeys.
- Problem statements.
- Experimental hypotheses.
- Prioritization.

### Stage 3 — Domain design

Define the financial concepts and interactions needed by each experiment.

Deliverables:

- Domain models.
- Financial data structures.
- Participant roles.
- Consent and authorization flows.
- API resource definitions.

### Stage 4 — Architecture

Translate the experiments into technical capabilities.

Deliverables:

- System architecture.
- Module boundaries.
- API specifications.
- Security architecture.
- Data architecture.

### Stage 5 — Implementation

Build the experimental platform using synthetic data and simulated participants.

Deliverables:

- Backend modules.
- Consumer Application.
- Bank Simulator.
- Developer Platform.
- Automated tests.
- Demonstrable workflows.

### Stage 6 — Advanced experimentation

Explore more sophisticated financial models and technologies.

Deliverables:

- AI experiments.
- Advanced interoperability.
- Embedded finance demonstrations.
- Programmable workflows.
- Autonomous agent experiments.

The stages can overlap when research and technical learning justify it.

## 11. Principles and guardrails

All Oquira experiments must respect the following principles.

### Dominican relevance

The Dominican Republic remains the primary context for research and experimentation.

### Open exploration

The project must investigate traditional institutions, fintech companies, emerging participants and future financial models.

### Evidence-based research

Documented facts must be distinguished from hypotheses, proposals and simulations.

### International learning

International standards and implementations are references for learning and adaptation, not automatic requirements.

### Synthetic data first

Initial experiments must use synthetic data and simulated institutions.

### Security by design

Identity, authorization, consent, privacy and auditability must be considered from the beginning.

### User control

Experiments involving financial information must demonstrate meaningful control over data access and permissions.

### Technical realism

Simulations should represent meaningful technical patterns without claiming real institutional connectivity or regulatory compliance.

### Independent experimentation

Oquira is not a bank, financial institution, regulated financial service or official Open Finance infrastructure.

Demonstrations of consent, identity and authorization do not constitute legal authorization to access real financial accounts.

## 12. Relationship with Oquira's architecture

The framework establishes the exploration scope. The technical architecture will provide the capabilities needed to experiment with it.

The initial platform will be organized around three principal experiences:

**Consumer Application**

An environment for exploring financial information, account aggregation, permissions and experimental financial services.

**Bank Simulator**

A simulated financial institution environment providing synthetic accounts, transactions, products and APIs.

**Developer Platform**

Documentation, API references, integration resources and tools for understanding the experimental ecosystem.

The backend will initially follow a modular monolith architecture, with explicit domain boundaries and shared infrastructure where appropriate.

Potential future modules include:

- Identity and Access.
- Consent Management.
- Financial Institutions.
- Accounts and Transactions.
- Payments.
- Credit.
- Financial Data Exchange.
- Notifications.
- Audit and Observability.
- AI and Financial Intelligence.

These are candidate capabilities, not a commitment to implement every module immediately.

## 13. Initial strategic priorities

Although the framework covers the entire financial ecosystem, implementation must be incremental.

The initial exploration priorities are:

1. Understand the Dominican financial ecosystem.
2. Investigate the current Open Banking and Open Finance landscape.
3. Study international reference architectures.
4. Define the financial institution and participant model.
5. Establish synthetic financial data structures.
6. Design identity, consent and authorization foundations.
7. Build the first simulated institution.
8. Demonstrate financial information aggregation.
9. Expand toward payments, credit, fintech and other financial domains.
10. Introduce advanced experiments as the platform matures.

Priorities may change as research reveals new opportunities.

## 14. Long-term direction

Oquira should evolve into a modular experimentation environment where researchers, developers and interested participants can explore how a more connected Dominican financial ecosystem might work.

The long-term objective is not to predict exactly how the Dominican financial system will evolve.

It is to create a practical environment for asking better questions, testing ideas, understanding technical possibilities and learning from different financial models.

**Oquira explores what exists, investigates what is possible and experiments with what could come next.**

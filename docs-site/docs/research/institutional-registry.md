# Dominican Financial Institutional Registry

**Version:** 0.1\
**Status:** Initial research\
**Scope:** Dominican Republic\
**Last reviewed:** November 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes a structured registry of the institutions and institutional categories that form or influence the Dominican financial ecosystem.

It is a research artifact for Oquira, not an official government directory, legal opinion or regulatory classification.

The registry will distinguish:

- Public authorities and policymakers
- Financial supervisors
- Financial market infrastructures
- Regulated financial institutions
- Cooperatives and community finance organizations
- Fintech companies and technology providers
- Other participants connected to financial services

## 2. Classification principles

Each registry entry should eventually contain:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Name | Official institutional or entity name |
| Category | Institutional classification |
| Role | Main function in the ecosystem |
| Supervisor | Relevant supervisory authority, if applicable |
| Legal basis | Applicable law or regulatory framework |
| Official source | Primary institutional source |
| Data relevance | Potential relevance to financial data exchange |
| Oquira relevance | Potential use in simulations or research |
| Evidence status | Fact, announced, in development, hypothesis, proposal or simulation |
| Last verified | Date of the latest source review |

An entity's inclusion in this research registry does not imply authorization to provide every financial service or participate in every payment or data-sharing system.

## 7. Open Finance research dimensions

For each institution or participant, Oquira should investigate:

1. What products and services does it provide?
2. What customer and business data does it generate?
3. Which data can the customer access?
4. Which data can be shared with third parties under applicable rules?
5. Does it publish APIs or developer documentation?
6. Does it support digital identity and authentication?
7. Does it participate in payment or settlement infrastructure?
8. What consent and authorization mechanisms exist?
9. Which regulatory restrictions apply?
10. What could be simulated in Oquira without connecting to real systems?

## 16. Historical Registry Dataset

### 16.1 Dataset overview

Oquira maintains a historical research dataset derived from the Superintendencia de Bancos (SB) open-data publication:

- Dataset: Dominican Financial Institutional Historical Registry
- Publisher: Superintendencia de Bancos de la República Dominicana
- Source period: January 2018 – September 2026
- Source format: CSV
- Historical observations: 11,455
- Processed historical records: 11,366
- Consolidated nominal identities: 186

The dataset records the presence of institution names under categories and monthly periods published by the source.

It is a historical research dataset. It must not be interpreted as a complete list of currently authorized, active or legally verified financial institutions.

### 16.2 Source provenance

The original source file is preserved without transformation:

- File: `data/registry/sb-authorized-entities-2018-2026.csv`
- Publisher: Superintendencia de Bancos de la República Dominicana
- Source URL: https://sb.gob.do/media/f4yh1a2h/listado-de-entidades-autorizadas-a-operar-2018-2026.csv
- SHA-256: `547A8D5C6357D525F93CD6A4F8F1478FEFC0C8ED34E6E0A6EA247FBCE41785DC`
- File size: 990,399 bytes

The downloaded source file is retained as an original research input. Processing must operate on this file without silently replacing or modifying its contents.

### 16.3 Temporal coverage

The current source snapshot covers January 2018 through September 2026.

The 2026 coverage is partial. No observations for October, November or December 2026 are represented in this snapshot.

The source file's local filesystem modification timestamp is not necessarily the publisher's publication date or the original download timestamp.

### 16.4 Dataset limitations

The dataset has the following limitations:

- Historical presence does not establish current authorization.
- Historical presence does not establish current operational status.
- A source name does not necessarily correspond to one verified legal entity.
- Names may change over time.
- Institutional categories and regulatory classifications may change.
- The source may contain historical records for institutions whose status has subsequently changed.
- The dataset does not currently contain verified legal identifiers or entity-level regulatory status histories.

The nominal identity count must not be compared directly with the current directory count without reconciling scope, dates and classification criteria.

## 17. Data Processing and Normalization

### 17.1 Processing pipeline

The initial processing pipeline is implemented in:

`scripts/registry/build-sb-registry.ps1`

The generator performs the following operations:

1. Reads the original SB CSV source.
2. Validates the presence of the required columns.
3. Excludes rows with missing entity, category, month or year values.
4. Groups exact entity, category, month and year combinations.
5. Preserves the number of source occurrences for each grouped record.
6. Validates month names and integer year values.
7. Sorts the historical records chronologically.
8. Normalizes entity names and categories.
9. Groups records by normalized name and category.
10. Generates deterministic nominal identifiers.
11. Calculates first and last observed periods.
12. Validates identifier uniqueness and observation totals.
13. Exports the historical CSV and institutional JSON catalog.

### 17.2 Normalization policy

The current normalization policy applies:

- Unicode normalization using FormKC.
- Uppercase invariant conversion.
- Leading and trailing whitespace removal.
- Consecutive whitespace consolidation.
- Category inclusion in the identity key.

The policy deliberately preserves:

- Punctuation.
- Legal suffixes.
- Abbreviations.
- Accents and other distinctions not removed by the defined normalization steps.

Approximate or fuzzy matching is not performed.

### 17.3 Identity model

Each catalog identity receives a deterministic identifier derived from:

`NormalizedCategory|NormalizedName`

The SHA-256 digest is truncated to its first 20 hexadecimal characters and prefixed with `sb-`.

These identifiers provide stable references within the generated nominal catalog. They are not official identifiers, regulatory registration numbers or verified legal-entity identifiers.

The catalog's identity policy is explicitly:

`Normalized source name and category; not a verified legal identity.`

### 17.4 Duplicate handling

The source contains repeated observations for some entity/category/month/year combinations.

The current processing consolidates exact duplicate combinations while preserving their original frequency in `SourceOccurrences`.

For the current snapshot:

- Original data rows: 11,456
- Valid observations: 11,455
- Processed historical records: 11,366
- Additional duplicate rows consolidated: 89
- Total preserved source observations: 11,455

The consolidated historical records must retain their occurrence counts. Deduplication must never silently discard source-frequency information.

Its provenance is represented by:

- File size in bytes.
- Local file modification timestamp, when recorded.

### 18.2 Historical dataset

`data/registry/processed/sb-institutional-history.csv`

Processed historical records with the following fields:

- `Entity`
- `Category`
- `Month`
- `Year`
- `SourceOccurrences`

Each row represents a unique entity/category/month/year combination after exact duplicate consolidation.

`SourceOccurrences` preserves the number of original source observations represented by each processed row.

### 18.3 Nominal institution catalog

`data/registry/processed/institutions.json`

Normalized nominal identity catalog.

Each institution record currently contains:

- `id`
- `name`
- `normalizedName`
- `category`
- `sourceNames`
- `firstObserved`
- `lastObserved`
- `observationCount`

The catalog currently contains 186 nominal identities.

The `sourceNames` field preserves observed spelling variants. These variants are not automatically interpreted as separate legal entities.

Identifiers are deterministic SHA-256-derived values based on normalized category and name. They are technical identity keys, not regulatory identifiers.

### 18.4 Source manifest

`data/registry/source-manifest.json`

The source manifest is the provenance and integrity record for the registry dataset.

It records:

- Dataset identification and source publisher.
- Original source URL and source file metadata.
- Source SHA-256 checksum and file size.
- Temporal coverage.
- Processing statistics and normalization policy.
- Known limitations and verification status.
- Generated artifact paths, sizes and SHA-256 checksums.
- Generator path and SHA-256 checksum.
- UTC generation timestamp and execution environment.

The manifest is automatically updated by the registry generator.

The `downloadedAt` field remains null unless an independently verified download timestamp becomes available.

The manifest does not contain its own checksum, avoiding a self-referential integrity calculation.

### 18.5 Generator

`scripts/registry/build-sb-registry.ps1`

The PowerShell generator recreates the historical dataset and nominal institution catalog from the preserved source file.

It performs:

1. Source file and required-column validation.
2. Incomplete-record filtering.
3. Exact historical duplicate consolidation.
4. Chronological ordering.
5. Unicode FormKC normalization.
6. Whitespace normalization and invariant uppercase conversion.
7. Nominal identity grouping by normalized name and category.
8. Preservation of original source-name variants.
9. Deterministic identifier generation.
10. Duplicate identifier detection.
11. Observation reconciliation.
12. Automatic source manifest and artifact metadata updates.

The generator can be executed from the repository root:

`./scripts/registry/build-sb-registry.ps1`

Source and generated artifact hashes are calculated using SHA-256.

The current implementation writes the historical dataset and catalog before updating the manifest. Full transactional generation and atomic replacement of all artifacts remain future engineering improvements.

## 19. Institutional Registry Architecture

Oquira separates institutional research into three related but independent data layers.

### 19.1 Historical Entity Registry

Purpose: preserve historical institutional observations from source datasets.

Core information:

- Source entity name.
- Source category.
- Observation period.
- Source occurrence count.
- Provenance and dataset version.
- Normalized nominal identity.

This layer answers:

"What institutional names appeared in the available historical sources, and when?"

### 19.2 Current Participant Registry

Purpose: describe participants identified in the Dominican financial ecosystem.

Its scope extends beyond entities supervised by the Superintendencia de Bancos.

Potential participant types include:

- Banks and other financial intermediaries.
- Savings and loan associations.
- Cooperatives and community finance organizations.
- Fintech companies.
- Payment service providers.
- Remittance and foreign exchange providers.
- Insurance and pension participants.
- Securities and investment participants.
- Financial infrastructure operators.
- Technology, identity and data service providers.
- Emerging and experimental financial participants.

Inclusion in this research layer does not imply regulatory authorization, endorsement or operational verification.

### 19.3 Regulatory and Operational Status

Purpose: represent the regulatory and operational status of participants using independently sourced evidence.

Status dimensions must remain distinct:

- Directory presence.
- Authorization status.
- Operational status.
- Administrative or liquidation status.
- Historical status.
- Unknown or pending verification.

Status records should eventually include:

- Participant identifier.
- Status type and value.
- Relevant authority.
- Evidence source.
- Effective date, when available.
- Verification date.
- Evidence quality.
- Reviewer or verification process.

A single generic `active` field is insufficient to represent these different dimensions.

### 19.4 Relationships between layers

The three layers may be linked when sufficient evidence supports the relationship.

A historical nominal identity must not automatically become a current participant or a verified legal entity.

Where identity matching is uncertain, the relationship must remain unresolved and be documented for review.

The three-layer model is a conceptual research architecture. It does not imply that all three layers have already been implemented as application services or database tables.

## 20. Data Governance and Verification

### 20.1 Evidence classification

Registry assertions should distinguish:

- **Fact:** directly supported by an identified source.
- **Announced:** publicly announced but not necessarily implemented.
- **In development:** implementation or development is documented.
- **Hypothesis:** an interpretation requiring validation.
- **Proposal:** a suggested future capability or structure.
- **Simulation:** an experimental representation inside Oquira.

These classifications describe the strength or nature of an assertion, not an institution's regulatory status.

### 20.2 Entity verification

Before treating a nominal identity as a verified legal entity, the research process should seek:

- Exact legal name.
- Official registration or institutional identifier.
- Competent supervisory or registration authority.
- Current authorization evidence, when applicable.
- Operational status evidence, when applicable.
- Effective dates and relevant status changes.
- Official source references.

Unverified information must remain explicitly marked as pending verification.

### 20.3 Source updates

Each new source snapshot should preserve:

- Original source file.
- Source URL and publisher.
- Retrieval date, when known.
- File hash.
- File size.
- Temporal coverage.
- Processing version.
- Processing statistics.
- Known exclusions and limitations.

New snapshots should not silently overwrite historical evidence.

Changes in source structure, classification or publication methodology must be documented before interpreting differences as institutional changes.

### 20.4 Corrections and reconciliation

Corrections should preserve the distinction between:

- Source transcription errors.
- Normalization decisions.
- Entity identity matches.
- Legal name changes.
- Regulatory status changes.
- Operational status changes.

A correction to a nominal identity must not erase the original source value.

### 20.5 Research status

The initial SB historical dataset is an exploratory research artifact.

Its nominal identities, legal identities and current regulatory or operational statuses remain separate concepts.

Independent entity-level verification and reconciliation against current official directories remain outstanding work.
